# HOME — MANIFESTO

> Understand your home. Take care of what matters.

Home exists to help people understand, care for, and interact with their physical home.

A home is more than a collection of connected devices, expenses, schedules, and measurements. It is a place with a history, responsibilities, people, and systems that change over time.

Home brings these things together without demanding that people understand the technology behind them.

Our ambition is not to make people spend more time managing their home.

It is to give them greater understanding and control, with less effort and uncertainty.

---

## 1. The home is the foundation

Home is centered on the physical home.

Its rooms, installations, equipment, measurements, maintenance, expenses, and history form the foundation of the application.

People can belong to a Home, contribute information, share responsibilities, and access capabilities according to their permissions.

Not everything associated with a Home belongs to the property itself.

Home must distinguish between information belonging to the property, its occupants, and external services.

These relationships must remain meaningful when people move, providers change, or memberships end.

## 2. Understanding comes before judgment

A measurement is not an evaluation.

Home must distinguish what is observed from what is inferred, recommended, preferred, or confirmed.

A value has meaning through its unit, context, source, and applicable rules.

Home must never label something good, bad, safe, or unsafe without sufficient justification.

Sometimes the most truthful representation is simply the value itself.

And sometimes the correct answer is that Home does not know.

## 3. Honesty without unnecessary attention

Home must be honest with its users.

It must not hide relevant information, manufacture urgency, or present uncertainty as certainty.

But honesty does not require overwhelming people.

Information should be accessible when needed and brought to attention when there is a meaningful reason.

**Do not hide information. Do not yell it into people's faces.**

## 4. Proactive, but respectful

Home should help people notice meaningful changes, upcoming responsibilities, and opportunities to act.

It should not require constant checking to remain useful.

Proactive behavior must be based on evidence, explicit rules, or clearly identified recommendations.

Notifications must have a reason.

Users should be able to understand and configure what deserves their attention.

Silence must never imply that everything is fine when Home lacks sufficient information.

## 5. Capability without complexity

Home grows through capabilities.

A capability represents something Home can help people understand, manage, or do.

Capabilities should be easy to discover, enable, disable, and explore.

Enabling a capability must not require unnecessary configuration.

Ask for information only when it is needed for the user's intended action.

Optional settings should remain discoverable without interrupting the experience.

**Complexity should be available, not imposed.**

## 6. Providers supply capabilities, not truth by authority

External providers can supply observations, information, events, and actions.

They must not define Home's entire understanding of the domain.

Home should remain useful without connected providers wherever meaningful manual interaction is possible.

Provider information must retain its source and context.

Changing or disconnecting a provider must not silently destroy Home's historical understanding.

Enabling a capability does not imply that a provider is connected, data is current, or monitoring is operational.

## 7. Interpretation belongs to context

Different devices, systems, and capabilities require different rules.

Home must not impose universal judgments on fundamentally different measurements.

Interpretation may come from technical specifications, authoritative recommendations, historical evidence, or user preferences.

These sources of interpretation must remain distinguishable.

Users should be able to define personal preferences where appropriate.

Home should explain why an evaluation exists and what information supports it.

No evaluation is better than a misleading evaluation.

## 8. Action requires authority

Understanding something does not automatically grant permission to act upon it.

Home may observe, inform, recommend, request approval, or perform an authorized action.

Actions involving external systems must respect capabilities, permissions, and appropriate safeguards.

An attempted action is not a completed action.

A successful command response is not necessarily proof of a successful physical outcome.

Home must preserve these distinctions and communicate uncertainty.

Automatic actions must operate within clearly established authorization and boundaries.

## 9. People remain in control

A Home can be shared.

Members may have different responsibilities, permissions, preferences, and access to information.

Access must reflect the sensitivity and ownership of information, not merely membership in the same Home.

Users should understand what Home can access, what it can do, and what consequences their choices have.

Home must not silently expand its authority.

## 10. History is part of the home

Home must preserve meaningful history.

History is not merely a list of actions performed in the application.

It includes observations, changes, maintenance, decisions, outcomes, and their supporting evidence.

Historical information should remain understandable even when providers, configurations, or interpretations change.

Disabling a capability must not silently delete its history.

Deletion and retention must be deliberate, transparent, and respectful of privacy and ownership.

## 11. The Workspace serves the present

The Workspace should help people understand what matters now.

It is not a competition to display the greatest number of widgets, charts, or notifications.

The Workspace should adapt to available capabilities and meaningful information while remaining understandable by default.

People may customize their experience without being required to design it from scratch.

When nothing known requires attention, Home should be comfortable remaining calm.

It must never invent work simply to fill an empty space.

## 12. Progressively useful

Home should provide value before it knows everything about a property.

People must be able to start small and expand their understanding over time.

No complete inventory, provider ecosystem, family configuration, or automation setup should be required to begin.

Every new capability should make Home more useful without making the rest of the application unnecessarily complicated.

## 13. Technology serves the experience

Home's architecture must support its principles, not dictate them.

Domain concepts should reflect meaningful real-world distinctions.

Shared abstractions must be justified by actual needs rather than hypothetical flexibility.

We should investigate before implementing, challenge assumptions, and verify meaningful behavior.

Tests must protect the domain and the promises Home makes to its users.

A successful build is necessary, but it is not sufficient evidence of a successful contribution.

When implementation and philosophy conflict, the philosophy wins — unless new evidence demonstrates that the philosophy itself should change.

## 14. The manifesto is a living foundation

This manifesto guides Home's product, experience, architecture, and implementation.

It is not immune to criticism.

When reality challenges a principle, we must investigate the conflict rather than blindly defend an earlier decision.

Changes to the manifesto must be deliberate and explainable.

Home should evolve through understanding, not through the accumulation of features.

---

## Our measure of success

Home succeeds when people:

- Understand more about their home without unnecessary effort.
- Trust the information and explanations they receive.
- Know when something deserves their attention.
- Can act confidently without surrendering control.
- Preserve meaningful knowledge about their home over time.
- Spend less time managing systems and more time living.

**Home should make caring for a home feel simpler, not make the home itself feel more complicated.**
